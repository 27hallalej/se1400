April 24, 2026

↓

The National Weather Service in Norman, Oklahoma has issued a preliminary rating of EF4 to the violent tornado which struck Enid, Oklahoma during the afternoon hours of April 23, 2026.

The environment was not necessarily anticipated to support such a violent tornado; mesoscale discussions less than an hour prior to the incident only forecast maximum intensities in the mid-EF3 range. According to KOCO News, no casualties were reported. Still, Vance Air Force Base and surrounding neighborhoods took heavy losses. Facilities at Vance AFB have begun to reopen, and the power has been fully restored.

The tornado reddened over Enid as it swept away homes, trees, and even the dirt from the ground.
Perhaps at the zenith of the tornado's strength, a well-built house was entirely swept away from its foundation.
Ground scouring was observed in the aftermath of the tornado, as seen from a bird's eye view. (Image courtesy of KOCO News)

This onslaught of severe weather is expected to continue for the next four days, centered mostly around Oklahoma and Kansas.

April 22, 2026

↓

The National Weather Service has issued the new Day 1-8 outlooks. Severe weather seems to be omnipresent throughout the next week, especially in 4 to 5 days, wherein a heightened Enhanced (30% chance of significant weather) risk has been issued.

This risk for severe weather should be very concerning for those in Oklahoma and Kansas. Stay weather aware!

April 20, 2026

↓

Today marks the beginning of a potentially significant severe weather event. Models are showing strong shear values and ample moisture return across the central Plains.

Surface dewpoints are expected to reach into the mid-60s°F, creating an environment conducive to supercell development. The main threat will be large hail and tornadoes, with the highest probability centered over Kansas and northern Oklahoma.

April 18, 2026

↑

A quiet weather pattern has settled over the region following last week's active severe weather. This break provides an excellent opportunity to review storm safety procedures.

Remember: when a tornado warning is issued, seek shelter immediately in a basement or interior room on the lowest floor. Stay away from windows and cover yourself with blankets or a mattress for protection.
